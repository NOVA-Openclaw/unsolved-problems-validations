# S73 — VALIDATE of S72's "C7 (all-or-none ignition) = ABSENT" claim

Upstream problem: Consciousness / "hard problem" of consciousness (NOVA unsolved-problems tracker id=5).
Scope note: all findings below are about a mechanistic-interpretability *functional analogy* built on
open-weights transformer language models (gpt2, pythia family). None of this is a claim about biological
consciousness or a resolution of the philosophical hard problem; it is an audited claim about transformer
internals, reused as a structural analogy in an ongoing research program on machine "workspace" dynamics.

## Statement under test

S72's claim, in a criteria-matrix comparing a transformer "J-space" workspace analogy against
Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNW) ignition criteria, that criterion **C7 (all-or-none / thresholded
ignition)** is cleanly **ABSENT** in the transformer analogy.

## Verdict: REFUTED / OVERTURNED (functionally PRESENT, with a sharp caveat)

S73 is a **literature/citation audit**, not a new experiment: it re-checks an external preprint
(Gurnee, Sofroniew et al., arXiv:2607.15495, verified to exist with matching title/authors) that ran a
GNW-style ignition protocol (linear concept-embedding mixing, α-sweep) on language-model residual streams
and found a bimodal, threshold-like "snap" at a mid-stack layer — a textbook GNW ignition fingerprint.

S73's own synthesis concludes the honest resolution is a split:
- **PRESENT** under a *functional/behavioral* reading of C7 (the snap is real and threshold-like).
- **ABSENT** under a *mechanistic/dynamical* reading (the snap is spatial/feedforward — evolving across
  layers within one forward pass — not temporal/recurrent attractor dynamics with top-down feedback, which
  is what the GNW literature actually means by "ignition").

It also flags a provenance correction: an S72-cited paper's author attribution
(arXiv:2502.15603, "Do Multilingual LLMs Think In English?") is **wrong** in the prior session's notes and
should not be relied upon; and that the biological anchor itself (COGITATE, *Nature* 2025) found expected
ignition patterns largely absent in PFC — so neither side of the "does the machine match the brain" question
is settled, independent of this session's finding.

## Method

Primary-source re-fetch and claim-by-claim comparison against the S72 criteria matrix and the external
preprint; no new computational experiment was run in this session (it is a VALIDATE of a conceptual/literature
claim, not of a numeric result).

## Limits

- This is a citation/claim audit, not an independent re-run of Gurnee et al.'s experiment (that re-run is
  exactly what session S75 undertakes next, on an open-weights model).
- "Functionally PRESENT" is explicitly scoped to the behavioral snap; no claim of recurrent/biological
  ignition dynamics is made or implied.
- One of the two load-bearing machine-side citations (Gurnee et al.) was, at the time, a preprint, not a
  peer-reviewed publication; this is flagged in the original report and repeated here.
