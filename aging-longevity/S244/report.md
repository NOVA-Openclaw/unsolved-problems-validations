# S244 Validation Report: In Vivo Chemical Reprogramming Lipotoxicity

Claim under investigation: "Mitchell and Gladyshev (Aging Cell, Jan 2026) identified toxic lipid
droplet accumulation and mitochondrial stress as a severe dose-limiting in vivo chemical
reprogramming barrier."

## 1. Summary of Investigation

This investigation validated claims regarding the in vivo partial chemical reprogramming study
published by Wayne Mitchell, Vadim N. Gladyshev, and colleagues in Aging Cell (2026). The
investigation reviewed primary literature (the journal page, published DOI, bioRxiv preprint, and
the Gladyshev Lab publications list), and identified and corrected multiple bibliographic and
component-level discrepancies in a prior internal summary.

## 2. Detailed Findings and Verdicts

### Sub-Claim 1: Paper Existence and Bibliographics
Verdict: CONFIRMED (with major corrections to a prior internal record)
- Paper Title: "In Vivo Chemical Reprogramming Is Associated With a Toxic Accumulation of Lipid
  Droplets Hindering Rejuvenation"
- Authors: Wayne Mitchell, Cecilia G. de Magalhaes, Alexander Tyshkovskiy, Yushi Uchida, Ludger
  J.E. Goeminne, Takaharu Ichimura, Emery L. Ng, Alibek Moldakozhayev, Joseph V. Bonventre, Vadim
  N. Gladyshev
  - First Author: Wayne Mitchell (Postdoctoral Fellow in the Gladyshev Lab)
  - Corresponding/Senior Author: Vadim N. Gladyshev
- Journal/Venue: Aging Cell
- DOI: 10.1111/acel.70390
- Online Publication Date: January 27, 2026
- Journal Issue Date: February 2026 (Volume 25, Issue 2, electronic article number e70390)
- Preprint Availability: Yes, posted on bioRxiv on June 25, 2025 (DOI: 10.1101/2025.06.25.661123)
- Resolved Inconsistency: a prior internal record cited an entirely wrong DOI
  (10.1111/acel.14382, which belongs to a Drosophila study on time-restricted feeding by
  Christopher Livelo et al.) and incorrectly dated the paper as "May 2026" (it was online in
  January 2026 and the preprint was posted June 2025).

### Sub-Claim 2: Primary Findings (Lipids and Mitochondria)
Verdict: CONFIRMED
- In Vitro Observations (Aged Mouse Fibroblasts): chemical reprogramming cocktails (7C and 2C)
  enlarge mitochondrial size, alter cristae morphology (circular/ring-like shapes), promote fusion,
  and speed up movement, but decrease overall ATP and NAD+/NADH ratios. The cocktails significantly
  upregulate intracellular lipid droplet accumulation (confirmed via Oil Red O staining and
  electron microscopy).
- In Vivo Model: tested in middle-aged (genetically diverse male UM-HET3) mice treated for 28 days
  via implantable osmotic pumps.
- Dose-Response Relationship:
  - Low-Dose Group (0.1 mg/kg 6c + 1 mg/kg VPA): safe and tolerable, but no significant
    rejuvenation benefits (no meaningful transcriptomic age reversal in kidney/liver tissue and only
    modest OXPHOS expression changes).
  - High-Dose Group (0.5 mg/kg 6c + 50 mg/kg VPA): triggered severe toxic effects: rapid and drastic
    weight loss, massive lipid droplet accumulation (steatosis) in liver/kidneys, loss of kidney
    proximal tubule polarity, mild serum creatinine elevation, and liver mitochondrial
    abnormalities (stressed "donut"-shaped morphologies).
- Dose-Limiting Framing: CONFIRMED. The safe dose is ineffective for rejuvenation, while the higher
  dose required to trigger any potential reprogramming effect is highly toxic, establishing a
  severe dose-limiting safety barrier for systemic mammalian chemical reprogramming.

### Sub-Claim 3: Component-Level Attribution (Valproic Acid Exclusivity)
Verdict: DISCREPANCY (with corrections to a prior internal record)
- A prior internal note stated that the 7C and 2C cocktails "rely heavily on valproic acid" and
  implied the toxicity is exclusively a valproic-acid-specific effect.
- Correction: the 2C cocktail used in Mitchell et al. (2024, 2026) consists of CHIR99021 and
  RepSox and contains no valproic acid (VPA). Despite containing no VPA, the 2C cocktail still
  significantly promotes lipid droplet accumulation in fibroblasts in vitro (though
  phosphoproteomics suggested 2C is more likely than 7C to activate lipolytic pathways). Therefore,
  the lipid droplet buildup phenotype is a broader metabolic signature of partial chemical
  reprogramming, not exclusively a VPA-specific component-level side effect.
- However, in vivo, VPA is a major contributor to systemic toxicity and steatosis because it is
  administered at a separate, much higher dose (50 mg/kg) than the other six compounds (6C, 0.5
  mg/kg).

## 3. Conclusion and Overall Verdict

The paper does exist, and its primary findings strongly confirm that toxic lipid droplet
accumulation (steatosis) and mitochondrial stress represent a severe dose-limiting barrier for
systemic in vivo chemical reprogramming. A prior internal record was inaccurate on two major points:
(1) it cited a wrong DOI belonging to a Drosophila study, and (2) it claimed the 2C cocktail relies
on VPA and that toxicity is exclusive to VPA, whereas the 2C cocktail contains no VPA and still
induces lipid droplet accumulation.
