# S246 VALIDATE: rentosertib peak arm

Claim under test: which arm carries the "~3-4 y" proteomic-age reduction in Zhavoronkov et al.,
Nat Biotech 2026 (doi 10.1038/s41587-026-03286-y). Candidates: 30 mg BID or 60 mg QD.

Method: Independent re-fetch of the Nature full text, cross-checked line by line against both
candidate attributions.

## Verdict: DISCREPANCY RESOLVED. The two arms lead on different measures.
- **30 mg BID is the most CONSISTENT and BROADEST arm.** It accounts for 9 of the 21 significant
  comparisons out of 54 total (Q<0.10, one-sided MWU, BH). It is the only arm detected by BOTH
  chronological and mortality clocks, and at week 4, 5 of 6 clocks show a negative Cohen's d. The
  main text gives NO magnitude in years for BID.
- **60 mg QD has the ONLY years-range stated in the main text:** deltaBioAge -2.71 to -3.46 y, across
  all 4 chronological clocks at week 4 (Q<0.10), with no significant change in either mortality
  clock. It had 7 significant comparisons. This is the arm with the best FVC result in the original
  trial report.
- 30 mg QD had 5 significant comparisons.
- The popular "3-4 years across 6 clocks" figure most likely comes from the 60 QD chronological-clock
  range. That range covers only 4 clocks, all chronological, and they are inter-correlated (r
  .78-.89). Attributing the years figure to BID was an error in an earlier internal note, now
  corrected.
- The organ-clock artery result (-6.95 to -16.57 y, mortality-based OrganAge variant, all arms and
  timepoints) is a separate, exploratory claim and is not part of the 6-clock headline.
- There is a week 4 to week 12 plateau: no arm-clock pair shifted significantly between weeks 4 and
  12.
- An earlier claim of "up to ~6 years" for BID was unsupported and has been removed.

## Implication for the thesis
The S245 discrepancy verdict gets stronger. The headline magnitude applies to one arm and one class
of clocks, so it cannot be read as broad multi-clock age reversal. Dose-schedule matters: BID beats
QD at the same total daily dose on breadth, which fits the steady-exposure hypothesis. That is a
hypothesis, not a finding (n approx 9-11 per arm).
