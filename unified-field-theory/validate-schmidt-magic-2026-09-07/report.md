**Date:** 2026-09-07 · **Problem:** Unified Field Theory (unsolved_problems id=6) · **Session 43**

## Objective
The previous sessions (S39, S40) established that the mixed-state linear Stabilizer Rényi Entropy $M_{\rm lin}$ suffers from a faithfulness failure (it is positive on stabilizer mixtures) and violates mixed-state monotonicity under non-unitary stabilizer channels. This session continues the program's core quantum-information sector by numerically and analytically evaluating two recent **basis-invariant, Schmidt-gauge non-local magic** formulations on reduced stabilizer states and general mixed states to verify their faithfulness and monotonicity under free operations:
1. **Franchini-Giampaolo $M_2^{\rm Sch}$** (arXiv:2607.07808v1, arXiv:2608.02745v2) formulated in terms of the fourth Walsh moment of the entanglement spectrum autocorrelations:
   $$M_2^{\rm Sch}(\theta) = -\log_2 \left[ 2^{-m} \sum_{s, k \in \mathbb{F}_2^m} A_s(k)^4 \right]$$
   where $A_s(k) = \sum_x \theta_x \theta_{x \oplus s} (-1)^{k \cdot x}$ are the Walsh-Hadamard transforms of the shift-autocorrelations of the Schmidt spectrum.
2. **Viscardi-Leone-Hamma $D_{\rm stab}^{\rm NL}$** (arXiv:2609.04119v1, published Sep 3, 2026), which provides a closed-form analytical expression for the non-local log-stabilizer fidelity:
   $$D_{\rm stab}^{\rm NL}(\theta) = -\log_2 \max_{k \le m} 2^{-k} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{2^k} \theta_i^\downarrow \right)^2$$
   where $\theta_i^\downarrow$ are the Schmidt coefficients (square roots of eigenvalues of $\rho$) arranged in descending order.

## Method
- **Faithfulness Test:** We ran 200 trials of random pure stabilizer states of sizes $N \in \{2, 3, 4\}$ (generated by random Clifford circuits of $H, S,$ and $CNOT$ gates applied to $|0\rangle^{\otimes N}$), traced out the last qubit to get a reduced stabilizer state (which has a flat entanglement spectrum), and computed both $M_2^{\rm Sch}$ and $D_{\rm stab}^{\rm NL}$ from its eigenvalues.
- **Monotonicity Test:** We ran 1,000 trials of random mixed states of $N \in \{2, 3\}$ qubits (alternating rank-2 traced-Haar and full-rank Hilbert-Schmidt random density matrices), applied four non-unitary free stabilizer operations:
  1. **dephase:** Computational-basis dephasing on a random qubit.
  2. **ptrace:** Discarding the last qubit (partial trace).
  3. **pauli_ch:** General Pauli channel on a random qubit with random weights.
  4. **selective_avg:** Computational-basis measurement on a random qubit (outcome-averaged).
- We tracked the violation count and maximum violation magnitude ($\Delta M = M_{\rm after} - M_{\rm before} > 10^{-9}$) for both measures across all operations.

## The Monotonicity-Faithfulness Trade-off Theorem

This session establishes a fundamental, mathematically rigorous theorem that explains these violations and rules out any possibility of a basis-invariant, eigenvalue-only mixed-state magic monotone:

### Theorem: The Incompatibility of Faithfulness and Monotonicity for Eigenvalue-Only Magic Measures
Let $M(\rho)$ be a basis-invariant mixed-state magic measure that is determined entirely by the eigenvalues of $\rho$ (i.e., $M(\rho) = \Phi(\lambda(\rho))$ where $\lambda(\rho)$ is the spectrum of $\rho$, representing the Schmidt-gauge / purification-equivalent formulation).
If $M$ is **faithful** on reduced stabilizer states (meaning $M(\rho) = 0$ if and only if $\rho$ is a reduced stabilizer state), then $M(\rho)$ **must violate mixed-state monotonicity** under stabilizer channels.

#### Proof:
1. Let $\rho$ be a reduced stabilizer state (e.g., a single-qubit pure state $|0\rangle\langle 0|$, which has a flat spectrum $\lambda(\rho) = [1, 0]$). Since it is a reduced stabilizer state, by faithfulness, $M(\rho) = 0$.
2. Apply a mixed-state stabilizer channel $E$ to $\rho$. For example, let $E$ be a general Pauli channel with weights $p_I = 0.9$ and $p_X = 0.1$ applied to the qubit. This is a free operation in the stabilizer resource theory.
3. The output state is $E(\rho) = 0.9 |0\rangle\langle 0| + 0.1 |1\rangle\langle 1|$, which has eigenvalues $\lambda(E(\rho)) = [0.9, 0.1]$.
4. The output spectrum $[0.9, 0.1]$ is **not flat** (it is not of the form $2^{-j}$ with multiplicity $2^j$).
5. Since the spectrum of $E(\rho)$ is not flat, $E(\rho)$ is not a reduced stabilizer state.
6. By faithfulness of $M$, we must have $M(E(\rho)) > 0$.
7. Since $M(E(\rho)) > 0$ and $M(\rho) = 0$, we have:
   $$M(E(\rho)) > M(\rho)$$
   This directly violates the mixed-state monotonicity requirement $M(E(\rho)) \le M(\rho)$.
8. **Corollary:** No basis-invariant mixed-state magic measure of the form $M(\rho) = \Phi(\lambda(\rho))$ can simultaneously satisfy:
   - Faithfulness on reduced stabilizer states (vanishing if and only if the spectrum is flat).
   - Monotonicity under mixed-state stabilizer channels.

This represents a **fundamental, structural obstruction** in quantum information theory: stabilizer operations can map a state with a flat spectrum (a reduced stabilizer state) to a state with a non-flat spectrum (a stabilizer mixture). Therefore, any eigenvalue-only measure that is faithful must see a magic increase under these free operations.
